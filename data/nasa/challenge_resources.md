# NASA Space Apps 2026: Health Monitoring Software (Resources tab)

Source: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/health-monitoring-software/?tab=resources
Read on 2026-11-14. The tab has **no downloadable datasets** (no CSV, API or data portal). All five resources are reference web pages, summarized below in our own words.

## NASA data & resources
1. **5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight**: https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/
   - The hazards are space radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, gravity fields, and hostile or closed environments (temperature, pressure, lighting, noise, microbes, CO2).
   - The hazards compound each other. They are studied with ground analogs, labs and the ISS.
2. **Risks of Human Spaceflight**: https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/risks/
   - Physiology: immune changes, sensorimotor/vestibular, cardiovascular, decompression sickness, host-microbe, hypoxia, food/nutrition, spacewalk injury (low O2 / high CO2), reduced cardio and musculoskeletal fitness, SANS (eyes/brain), bone loss.
   - Behavioral: behavioral/psychiatric, teamwork, sleep and irregular schedules, systems-operations support, injury from dynamic loads.
   - Radiation: radiation-induced cancer.
3. **Ongoing HRP Research**: https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/ongoing-hrp-research/
   - Artemis II: ARCHeR (well-being, activity, sleep monitoring), Immune Biomarkers, Standard Measures.
   - LEO: B-Complex, Brain Fluid Pressure, CIPHER, manual piloting, occupant protection, spinal fluid biomarkers, Standard Measures, Thigh Cuff, Venous Haemostasis, Zero T2 (exercise).
   - Past: Altered Immunity, Food Acceptability/Physiology, Omics Archive, Space Gardening, Spinal Health, Tempus Pro (telemedicine vitals device), Veggie Monitoring.

## Space agency partner resources (CSA)
4. **Monitoring Radiation for Astronaut Health**: https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/news/articles/2026/2026-02-26-monitoring-radiation-astronaut-health.asp
   - CANS (Canadian Active Neutron Spectrometer) is a compact, autonomous, continuous neutron-dose instrument. Neutrons are about 30% of total ISS radiation. Earlier instruments were MOSFET, EVARM and Radi-N/N2.
5. **Why Do We Conduct Science Experiments in Space?**: https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/iss/science/why-do-we-conduct-science-experiments-in-space.asp

## Signals worth mapping into the VITALX engine
Heart rate / HRV (cardiovascular), SpO2 (hypoxia), cabin CO2 / O2 / pressure / temperature (closed environment), radiation dose rate (incl. neutrons), sleep duration and timing (sleep risk), activity / exercise minutes (fitness, bone), and mood / workload self-report (behavioral).

Real telemetry is not linked from this page. The closest NASA open data is OSDR (osdr.nasa.gov), for example Rodent Research EDA environmental telemetry CSVs.
